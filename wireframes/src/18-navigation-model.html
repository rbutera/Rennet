Navigation model: the wayfinding structure

The map · new
the structure map (v4.0) spine · tree · law · history

The model v4.0 is named for. Frame 17 draws the forward journey; this draws the structure: the hybrid spine (a title-bar breadcrumb for "where am I", a left nav rail with back/forward for "how did I get here", and the palette for "where can I go"), the full destination tree with every place tagged peer / child / overlay, the tab-vs-crumb-vs-overlay law, the history keys, and one-review-at-a-time. Pure UX — it only ever adds reachable places; it never interposes a gate.

4The law: tab vs crumb vs overlay
Lens peer · tab
Files · Spec · Sequence · Decisions · Flagged · Noise are tabs on one lens bar. Switching one never moves the crumb — you have not gone anywhere.
Surface child · crumb
Project → review → Draft → Paper each extend the crumb by a segment. Esc or the crumb ascends one. These are the moves back/forward record.
Overlay neither
Conversation, inspector, palette, Settings-return: they touch neither crumb nor history. They open over where you are and close back to it.

Designer notes  /  what to weigh in on
1
Hybrid spine, both halves. The left nav rail is where you GO (back/forward · Home · Projects · the current lineage); the title-bar breadcrumb is where you ARE. Sidebar = where can I go; breadcrumb = where am I. Not either/or.
2
Back is a different verb from up. The crumb is hierarchical up (ascend a tier); back/forward (⌘[ / ⌘]) is temporal undo of navigation over surfaces. Both drawn, because they answer different questions.
3
Peer / child / overlay is the whole grammar. A lens is a peer tab (no crumb move); a surface is a child (extends the crumb, recorded in history); an overlay — Ask, inspector, palette, Settings-return — touches neither. Every destination in the app fits one bucket.
4
Reachability only — no gates. Nothing here confirms, warns, or blocks. Back from a draft mid-edit just navigates away and the draft persists (state preservation, not are-you-sure). Wayfinding adds places; it never adds ceremony (Rule Zero).
