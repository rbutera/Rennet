What is where

The bench is a five-column rail across the top of the main region, one column per lens; Flagged's column carries two lines because it has two voices. Under the rail the main region splits: the selected lane's board on the left, that same lane's transcript on the right. The chat dock is left alone and keeps showing the review's own conversation — this is the only option where you can watch a seat and still see what you asked the orchestrator.

The letters a–d tie a receipt to the element it wrote; in a built version that is a shared highlight on hover plus a one-shot pulse as the element lands. Heavier borders mark the element and receipt that just arrived; dashed boxes are where the next ones go.

On click

Clicking a rail column selects that lens and swaps both panes together — board and transcript can never disagree about which lens you are on. Flagged's column has two hit targets, one per voice: picking either shows the one Flagged board on the left and that voice's thread on the right. The top bar's lens switcher stays and does the same thing, so a lens has two controls. The two panes are independently scrollable, and the split can be dragged. Closing the chat dock widens both panes; it does not affect which transcript is shown, because the transcript no longer lives in the dock.

Cost against the current shell

Medium, and the cost is concentrated in one place: the transcript has to render outside the chat dock. Today packages/app-ui/src/chat/t3-chat-dock.tsx is the single mount of the T3 slot (slot.thread / slot.session from t3-chat-slot.tsx), and it is mounted once for the whole app. A second live thread view in the main region is new ground, and it is the risk in this option.

packages/app-ui/src/shell/top-bar.tsx needs a decision it does not have today: its LEFT slot hides the trail whenever the dock is open (chatOpen ? null : <Trail/>) on the rule that the trail belongs to the leftmost pane. That rule still holds here, but the top bar's chat toggle now opens a pane that is not the transcript, which is a different promise than the one the bench makes today. preparation-bench.tsx loses its five-reader scene to a rail, and its openLensThread call would now point a main-region pane rather than the dock — the store slice (ui.lensThread) can stay exactly as it is.
