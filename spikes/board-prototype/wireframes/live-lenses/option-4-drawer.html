What is where

The main region is the bench and nothing else: five lane rows, top to bottom, with Flagged's two voices as indented rows under it. The selected lane's row expands into a drawer that sits between it and the next lane. Inside the drawer, the board is on the left and that same lane's transcript is on the right; the drawer is the only place either appears, so there is no way to be looking at one lens's board beside another lens's transcript.

Letters a–d tie a receipt to the element it wrote — a shared highlight in a built version, plus a pulse as the element lands. Heavier borders mark what just arrived; dashed boxes mark where the next thing goes. The lane rows above and below the drawer keep their live status lines, so the other four lenses stay legible while you read one.

On click

Clicking a lane row opens its drawer and closes whichever was open — one drawer at a time, so the page has one focus. Clicking the open lane's row again collapses it back to a rail of five rows. Flagged's drawer holds one board and a two-tab transcript pane (Claude / Codex); switching voice changes only the right-hand pane. The top bar's lens switcher still selects a lens, which while a drawer is open means two controls for one thing — the honest fix is to have the switcher open the matching drawer.

Cost against the current shell

Low to medium. Nothing in packages/app-ui/src/shell/top-bar.tsx changes, and packages/app-ui/src/app/preparation-bench.tsx keeps the whole outlet — its five-reader scene survives as the rail, and its per-seat voice model (voicesOf) is exactly what the two Flagged rows and the two transcript tabs need. The bench already renders boards through LensBoardDocument, so putting one inside a drawer is a move, not a new capability.

Two real costs. The transcript has to render outside the chat dock, and packages/app-ui/src/chat/t3-chat-dock.tsx is currently the single mount of the T3 slot. And the drawer is a narrow, nested reading surface: a board written for a full-width document column has to survive being shown at roughly half the main region's width inside a row, which is the shape most likely to feel cramped once a board is long.
