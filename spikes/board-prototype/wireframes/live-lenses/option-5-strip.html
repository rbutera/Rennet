What is where

The board fills the main region and is marked provisional by the in progress chip and the ghost row at its end. Along the bottom sits the strip: six tabs for five lenses, because Flagged's two voices are two threads and therefore two tabs. Each tab carries its seat's state and elapsed time; the strip body below the tabs is that seat's live transcript. The strip is the bench — there is no separate bench screen.

Letters a–d tie a receipt to the element it wrote; in a built version that is a shared highlight across the board/strip boundary, and an element pulses once as its receipt lands. Because board and strip are stacked rather than side by side, the tie has to work vertically — an element at the top of the board and its receipt at the bottom of the strip can be a long way apart.

On click

Clicking a tab selects that seat: the strip body swaps to its transcript and the board above swaps to that seat's lens. The two Flagged tabs both show the Flagged board — the board does not change between them, only the transcript, which is the one case where the strip and the board are not one-to-one. The top bar's lens switcher still selects a board; picking a lens there moves the strip to that lens's first seat. “Collapse strip” hides the transcript and gives the board the full height; the tabs stay as a one-line status bar, so the five lanes never leave the screen.

Cost against the current shell

Highest of the five. The strip is a new region in the app layout — today the main region is one column under packages/app-ui/src/shell/top-bar.tsx, and a persistent bottom pane is new structure. packages/app-ui/src/app/preparation-bench.tsx is demoted to a tab row: the five-reader scene, the marks, the lanterns and the gaze lines all lose their stage, which is most of that file. The transcript again has to mount outside packages/app-ui/src/chat/t3-chat-dock.tsx, the single mount of the T3 slot.

What it buys: seat selection is one control and it is always visible, the board keeps its full width, and Flagged's two voices are first-class rather than a sub-choice inside a lane. What it costs beyond the code: permanent vertical space in a window that already spends 56px on the top bar, and a receipt-to-element tie that has to reach across a horizontal split.
